Which articles belong to which check? The 500,000 records are the starting collection.
The text experiment uses 52,000 of them to compare title, abstract and both; its earlier
26,000-paper trial is included, not added. The geometric analysis in S6–S7 reuses that same
set of 52,000. A diﬀerent set of 52,000 is used for the shared-passage check in S1. The
matched searches in S4 follow 10,850 starting papers while changing the papers available
around them. These are overlapping uses of one collection, not separate downloads.
Here, Field means one of the 26 broad research areas and Subﬁeld means a narrower spe-
cialty. The main article introduces the comparisons in that order; this supplement keeps the
database names and technical deﬁnitions so that the calculations can be checked.

S1 Corpus, models and provenance
S1.1 Eligibility, quotas and quality ﬂags
OpenAlex’s core catalogue was queried for 2000–2024 records of type article, review or
conference-paper, with an English language label, an abstract, no retraction or paratext
ﬂag and a known primary Field. Local preparation required a non-empty title and at least
50 regex words in the reconstructed abstract. It preserved literal content rather than trans-
lating or generating missing summaries. The unit was a distinct OpenAlex work ID, not a
deduplicated intellectual work.
Seeded random request blocks supplied the general cohort. Saved candidates were processed
in their recorded order until 400,000 eligible, distinct IDs were retained. The 100,000-record
supplement was assigned to the smallest of the 130 Field-period groups, with deterministic
Field/year tie-breaking. This raised minimum coverage without reducing the larger groups.
Exact year counts were retained rather than forcing equal quotas within each ﬁve-year period.
Raw retrieval ended on 15 September 2026; API responses were collected over time, so the
corpus is not a simultaneous database snapshot. Saved responses, ID order and content
hashes are required to reconstruct it; seeds alone would not reproduce a changing API.
Cleaning rejected recognised non-abstract content, explicit withdrawal/retraction titles and
clear language failures. For language, fastText’s lid.176 model was applied to the whole
abstract and three consecutive parts. A record was excluded only if the whole text had a
non-English top label with score at least 0.9 and all three parts agreed on that language
with scores at least 0.8. These are conservative operational scores, not calibrated probabil-
ities or independent tests. Remaining uncertainty, possible mixed language, 50–79-word or
unusually long abstracts, possible notices and duplicate DOI/text were marked. Content
rules were developed by inspecting the downloaded corpus before embedding; they were not
preregistered before extraction.
The ﬁnal corpus retains 9,883 abstracts with language uncertainty, including 3,002 ﬂagged as
potentially mixed. These sets overlap. It also includes 42,849 abstracts of 50–79 words and
249 over 2,000 words. Subsequent analysis uses the ﬂags for sensitivity rather than silently
changing the corpus. Source checks establish agreement with downloaded records, not that
every record is correct. Table S1 supplies the distribution and the full ﬂag tables.

S1.2 Checkpoints, input and truncation
All ten models use the same ordered IDs and original title/abstract text. The customary
separators, full model revisions and any adapter revisions are listed in Table S2. Outputs
were saved as 32-bit vectors; subsequent geometric calculations use 64-bit arithmetic. Miss-
ing or degenerate outputs were checked, and each run records code, conﬁguration, ﬁle and
environment hashes.
Mean pooling averages every non-padding last-layer position, including model-added special
tokens. CLS is the ﬁrst-position state before any pooler; SEP is the ﬁnal non-padding posi-
tion, rather than the separator between title and abstract. Only BERT, SciBERT, BioBERT
and PubMedBERT change under the CLS/SEP scenarios. SimCSE uses its pre-pooler CLS
state; SPECTER2 uses its proximity adapter. MPNet and MiniLM retain their sentence-
transformer mean-pooling conﬁguration. Readout scenarios are not additional independently
trained models.
The common-fragment comparison ﬁnds a shared preﬁx of content characters that ﬁts every
model, keeping each tokenizer and its customary separators. It uses a separate 52,000-ID
panel and compares that fragment with the native representation of those same IDs. It
does not equate token sequences between vocabularies. MiniLM’s separate 512-token check
uses its supported architectural limit; vectors for texts unaﬀected by the original truncation
are reused exactly. Neither check demonstrates that longer input gives better thematic
representations.

S2.3 Selection screens and retained alerts
The original screen uses twenty selections at each of 1,024 and 2,048 articles per Field-period
group, paired between models. It ﬂags an absolute median shift above 0.02 or a central 95%
selection width above 0.04 at the larger size. A row permutation per model/group provides an
alignment diagnostic rather than a permutation-test p-value. The quality sensitivity retains
one representative per connected duplicate DOI/text component and excludes speciﬁed ﬂags,
leaving 448,886 records. It changes the operational population and does not replace the main
corpus.
Subﬁeld screens use twenty nested selections up to 256/512 articles, subject to availability.
Groups below eight articles lack a two-size diagnosis. If the larger draw equals the full
group, its zero selection width is explicitly marked and cannot establish precision for a wider
population. Table S4 keeps these cases separate from the original Field-period alerts.

S3 Text input, pooling and truncation
This experiment keeps the papers ﬁxed and changes what each model reads. The main
question is whether that change matters more or less than choosing another model.
S3.1 Model-speciﬁc input eﬀects
The title/abstract experiment uses 52,000 articles: 400 per Field-period group and 2,000 per
Field, selected by a ﬁxed ID-based order. The ﬁrst trial used 26,000; all those IDs remain
in the larger set. For each model, the model-change eﬀect is one minus mean agreement
with the other nine models on title plus abstract. The input-change eﬀect is one minus
agreement between that model’s combined input and its title-only or abstract-only input.
Their diﬀerence is positive when model change is larger. Neighbour changes use the same
2,000 candidates per Field; self is excluded.
A symmetric summary of input transitions checks dependence on the combined-input refer-
ence. Mean, CLS and SEP scenarios cross all three inputs; only the four word-level BERTs
change readout, which also changes the cross-model reference. Saved combined-input and pi-
lot vectors were reused with exact ID/text alignment and bitwise checks. The analysis makes
a practical comparison of input conditions, not a causal decomposition of architecture and
content.

S4 Scale, neighbourhoods and concrete cases
These checks move from broad areas to specialties and individual papers. They separate a
change in search scope from a change in the number of available papers.
S4.1 Matched scopes and candidate sizes
Within-group comparisons use 128, 256 and 512 articles, with nested ID-based selections and
the same 183 eligible Subﬁelds at all three sizes. The native analysis retains all 252 Subﬁelds
where the measure is deﬁned; groups with exactly k + 1 articles have uninformative complete
neighbour lists and are marked accordingly. A separate time comparison retains the same
125 Subﬁelds with at least 128 articles in every period.
Centre comparisons average unit article vectors and scale each centre to unit length before
corrected CKA. Direct and weighted-Subﬁeld reconstructions of Field centres were checked
for equality. The original matched centres contain 256 articles per group. Its twenty re-
stricted selections change which Subﬁeld represents each Field, not the articles within those
Subﬁelds. The repeated centre, size, matched-random and omission analyses now reported

S6 Geometric properties and construct checks
These checks ask what a numerical property actually describes. A measure can repeat reliably
and still fail to distinguish separate groups from an elongated cloud or a few extreme points.
S6.1 Properties, alternatives and selection stability
The geometric analysis reuses the 52,000-article input panel, with 2,000 articles per Field and
equal period counts. All vectors have unit length and calculations use 64-bit arithmetic. For
distinct vectors ui, u j, angular spread uses the median pairwise chord distance, converted

quantiles before this monotone conversion; the same convention is retained throughout. Al-
ternatives are its 10th/90th percentiles and the median angle to the centre direction. They
describe spread rather than semantic diversity or isotropy ( Rudman et al. , 2022).

S6.3 Representation and content controls
Controls vary quality ﬂags, title/abstract input, readout, common content, extreme points,
global centring and the MiniLM token limit. The quality comparison replenishes each period
to its speciﬁed size after applying recorded ﬂags. The extreme-point control removes the ten
points farthest from the centre in each period of the initial half sample; removing ten random
points per period supplies its equal-size reference. It does not presume that extreme points
are erroneous.
Global centring subtracts the mean of the balanced 52,000 unit vectors and then normalises
each result again. This changes the representation and is distinct from centring a covariance
matrix for PR. Three Gaussian clouds per model–Field case provide geometric references
with matching mean/covariance only in expectation before unit normalisation. Their re-
alised moments are checked; these references are neither exact matched-moment controls nor
calibrated statistical null distributions. The point controls in Figure S8 and Table S16 have
their own matched references and must not be treated as another complete set of repeated
selections.

S6.4 Why connection is not general fragmentation
Connection uses the second-smallest eigenvalue of the normalised graph Laplacian L =
I − D−1/2AD−1/2 on the unweighted union of directed nearest-neighbour links. The value is
zero for a disconnected graph. Primary k = 25 is checked at 10 and 50, with a weighted local-
scale graph and mutual-neighbour components as alternatives. Spectral-clustering theory
motivates the construction ( von Luxburg , 2007); it does not establish that a small gap
means separate scientiﬁc themes. Eigenvalue residuals and independent dense calculations
were checked.
Single-linkage merging supplies the radii at which the largest component reaches speciﬁed
fractions of the cloud. Radius ratios and the maximum-to-median minimum-spanning-tree
edge ratio provide diﬀerent connection descriptions. Nine synthetic clouds, followed by Gaus-
sian references and article controls, exposed non-equivalent responses to elongation, isolated
outliers and separated components. Fixed k also changes its fraction of candidates as sample
size grows. The approximately ﬁxed-fraction comparison was added during execution and
is identiﬁed as a later sensitivity. These failures are why connection is excluded from the
manuscript’s interpretation of Field fragmentation.

S8.1 Rebuilding this display package
The repository command ./manuscript/build.sh compiles the included LaTeX into the
main document and this supplement. It does not run models, searches or simulations. The
compiler may download standard TeX support ﬁles on its ﬁrst use. Re-exporting plots and

S8.2 Access limits of this draft
The package provides the editable manuscript, ﬁgures, descriptive tables and their source
summaries. It excludes corpus text, vectors, model weights, secrets and copies of third-party
full texts. It can therefore reproduce the two displayed PDFs without reproducing the entire
scientiﬁc pipeline. The latter requires the frozen inputs and execution materials listed above.
A versioned public release and any necessary data permissions/licence must be settled before
claiming full public reproducibility. No persistent deposit has yet been made.
The author has conﬁrmed sole authorship as an independent researcher, no external funding
and no competing interests. The current prose and scientiﬁc interpretations remain a draft
for author review. Correspondence details and ﬁnal journal formatting should be completed
at submission; they have not been invented here.

S9 Final targeted robustness checks
These checks were speciﬁed after the previous results and the draft, before their new outcomes
were inspected. They reuse the frozen vectors, preserve all earlier outputs and address the
remaining selection, processing and source-quality questions. They add no model training

S9.1 Repeated centres and direct neighbour contrasts
The 256-article centre check uses ﬁfty selections of the same 26 Fields and 217 Subﬁelds.
Within each group, selection preserves the original ﬁve period counts. The size check ﬁxes 26
Fields and 183 Subﬁelds at 128, 256 and 512 articles; each has ﬁfty repetitions. Period quotas
derive from the original 512-article preﬁx, with nested largest-remainder rounding for smaller
sizes. Thus its 256-article quotas diﬀer from the ﬁrst check. One within-period reassignment
accompanies every selection, using exactly the same articles across observed/random groups
and models.
The restricted comparison selects one eligible Subﬁeld per Field ﬁfty times, then selects
articles ten times within each choice. Between-choice standard deviation of the observed
mean is 0.01205; mean within-choice standard deviation is 0.00157. These are descriptive
components, not independent population variances. The old restricted random reference
drew 26 centres from a reassignment across all 217 Subﬁelds; the new reference instead
shuﬄes exactly the selected 26 groups’ articles. Omitting each Field from the original centres
changes mean CKA by at most 0.00687, for Mathematics; the largest individual model-pair
shift is 0.02355 for MiniLM–BioBERT. All omissions are also calculated across the ﬁfty new
selections.
For direct neighbour stability, each of ﬁfty selections draws 200 papers per Field and period
from the text experiment: 1,000 candidates per Field. Queries and candidates coincide;
self matches are excluded. Full exact candidate rankings can be restricted to these subsets
without changing the search. Independent direct sorting checks 4,680 queries, in addition to
synthetic checks with tied vectors. The contrast averages the ten same-model title changes
and the 45 between-model title-plus-abstract changes, weighting Fields equally. A model-
speciﬁc reference averages its nine model comparisons. Main k values are 10, 25 and 50;
CLS/SEP aﬀect only the four word BERTs and are checked at k25. Model-speciﬁc mean
contrasts range from − 14. 04 to +16. 59 percentage points; Field means range from − 4. 43 to
+5. 63. The grand mean is not a universal ordering or an equivalence test.

S9.2 Quality ﬁltering with matched queries
The existing strict ﬁlter removes seven ﬂagged categories and retains the lowest row index
in each connected component of identical nonempty DOI or normalised text. This is not
a new near-duplicate detector. It retains 448,886 records. Full searches compare every
surviving query against original and ﬁltered candidates in its Field-period group. At k25,
the original all-query mean is 30.29%; restricting queries alone gives 30.58%, and ﬁltering
their candidates gives 31.43%. The matched-query gain is 0.85 points, or 0.77 after the
existing chance adjustment.
The equal-count controls include the same 100 clean queries in both candidate pools, ﬁlling
remaining places by deterministic random order. All 130 cells support 1,024 candidates;
only 63 support 2,048. The largest Field-average change in full searches is 2.72 points in
Mathematics. Across model pairs, the largest mean gain is 1.43 points for SPECTER2–
SciBERT. The 52,000-paper text panel has at least 235 clean papers per Field-period cell.
It supports 200 candidates and twenty clean queries per period in both arms, or 1,000
candidates and 100 queries per Field. Thus candidate counts, dates and queries stay matched.

S9.3 Complete processing and dimension checks
For each model, the global mean is calculated once from its 52,000 unit vectors. Every original
unit vector in each saved selection has that mean subtracted, then is normalised again. The
mean is not recalculated per Field or repetition. Covariance centring alone would leave PR
unchanged; the subsequent row normalisation makes this a diﬀerent representation. Spread
and PR use the original main selection, twenty halves and ﬁve further equal-size selections.
Entropy dimension and D80 are completed on those same 26 original selections. No selection
or magnitude cutoﬀ is changed.
