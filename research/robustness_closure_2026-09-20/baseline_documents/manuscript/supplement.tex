\textbf{Which articles belong to which check?}
The 500,000 records are the starting collection. The text experiment uses 52,000 of them to compare title, abstract and both; its earlier 26,000-paper trial is included, not added. The geometric analysis in S6--S7 reuses that same set of 52,000. A different set of 52,000 is used for the shared-passage check in S1. Here, Field means one of the 26 broad research areas and Subfield means a narrower specialty. The main article introduces the comparisons in that order; this supplement keeps the database names and technical definitions so that the calculations can be checked.

\section{Corpus, models and provenance}
\subsection{Eligibility, quotas and quality flags}
OpenAlex's core catalogue was queried for 2000--2024 records of type article, review or conference-paper, with an English language label, an abstract, no retraction or paratext flag and a known primary Field. Local preparation required a non-empty title and at least 50 regex words in the reconstructed abstract. It preserved literal content rather than translating or generating missing summaries. The unit was a distinct OpenAlex work ID, not a deduplicated intellectual work.

Seeded random request blocks supplied the general cohort. Saved candidates were processed in their recorded order until 400,000 eligible, distinct IDs were retained. The 100,000-record supplement was assigned to the smallest of the 130 Field-period groups, with deterministic Field/year tie-breaking. This raised minimum coverage without reducing the larger groups. Exact year counts were retained rather than forcing equal quotas within each five-year period. Raw retrieval ended on 15 September 2026; API responses were collected over time, so the corpus is not a simultaneous database snapshot. Saved responses, ID order and content hashes are required to reconstruct it; seeds alone would not reproduce a changing API.

Cleaning rejected recognised non-abstract content, explicit withdrawal/retraction titles and clear language failures. For language, fastText's lid.176 model was applied to the whole abstract and three consecutive parts. A record was excluded only if the whole text had a non-English top label with score at least 0.9 and all three parts agreed on that language with scores at least 0.8. These are conservative operational scores, not calibrated probabilities or independent tests. Remaining uncertainty, possible mixed language, 50--79-word or unusually long abstracts, possible notices and duplicate DOI/text were marked. Content rules were developed by inspecting the downloaded corpus before embedding; they were not preregistered before extraction.

The final corpus retains 9,883 abstracts with language uncertainty, including 3,002 flagged as potentially mixed. These sets overlap. It also includes 42,849 abstracts of 50--79 words and 249 over 2,000 words. Subsequent analysis uses the flags for sensitivity rather than silently changing the corpus. Source checks establish agreement with downloaded records, not that every record is correct. Table S1 supplies the distribution and the full flag tables.

\subsection{Checkpoints, input and truncation}
All ten models use the same ordered IDs and original title/abstract text. The customary separators, full model revisions and any adapter revisions are listed in Table S2. Outputs were saved as 32-bit vectors; subsequent geometric calculations use 64-bit arithmetic. Missing or degenerate outputs were checked, and each run records code, configuration, file and environment hashes.

Mean pooling averages every non-padding last-layer position, including model-added special tokens. CLS is the first-position state before any pooler; SEP is the final non-padding position, rather than the separator between title and abstract. Only BERT, SciBERT, BioBERT and PubMedBERT change under the CLS/SEP scenarios. SimCSE uses its pre-pooler CLS state; SPECTER2 uses its proximity adapter. MPNet and MiniLM retain their sentence-transformer mean-pooling configuration. Readout scenarios are not additional independently trained models.

The common-fragment comparison finds a shared prefix of content characters that fits every model, keeping each tokenizer and its customary separators. It uses a separate 52,000-ID panel and compares that fragment with the native representation of those same IDs. It does not equate token sequences between vocabularies. MiniLM's separate 512-token check uses its supported architectural limit; vectors for texts unaffected by the original truncation are reused exactly. Neither check demonstrates that longer input gives better thematic representations.

\section{Agreement measures and sampling stability}
This section explains how agreement between two models is calculated, then checks how much the result changes when other articles are selected. Those are two different questions.

\subsection{Coverage and chronology}
\input{tables/tex/S03}
\subsection{Alignment and neighbour definitions}
For each comparison, rows of $X$ and $Y$ identify the same articles. Vectors are scaled to unit length, and columns are then centred for CKA and Procrustes. Let $n$ be the row count and define
\begin{align*}
S_{XY}&=\lVert X^\top Y\rVert_F^2, &
R_{XY}&=\sum_i\lVert X_i\rVert^2\lVert Y_i\rVert^2,\\
T_X&=\sum_i\lVert X_i\rVert^2, &
H_{XY}&=S_{XY}-\frac{n}{n-2}R_{XY}
+\frac{T_XT_Y}{(n-1)(n-2)}.
\end{align*}
The reported corrected alignment is
\[
\mathrm{CKA}_{\mathrm{corr}}(X,Y)=\frac{H_{XY}}{\sqrt{H_{XX}H_{YY}}}.
\]
The common HSIC factor cancels. Negative corrected values are retained, and degenerate inputs are rejected. The implementation was checked independently using relation matrices with zero diagonals. The correction addresses finite-sample inflation \citep{pmlr-v97-kornblith19a,refa961d84a4e}; the complete ratio is not asserted to be exactly unbiased.

Procrustes similarity is $\lVert X^\top Y\rVert_*/\sqrt{T_XT_Y}$, where the numerator is the sum of singular values. Its arccosine gives the reported angular distance. Rotation/reflection and a global scale can be aligned; an arbitrary deformation cannot. Unequal embedding dimensions can be understood by adding zero coordinates to the smaller representation. Normalisation precedes centring, so this procedure does not imply invariance to translating the original vectors and normalising them again \citep{NEURIPS2021_252a3dba}.

The distance-rank check correlates cosine distances for 20,000 distinct article pairs selected with a recorded seed, identically in both representations. Ties receive average ranks. These pair observations and the alternative alignment measures are not treated as independent replications \citep{pmlr-v285-williams24a}.

For query $i$ and models $a,b$, neighbour overlap is
\[
O_k(i;a,b)=\frac{|N_k^a(i)\cap N_k^b(i)|}{k}.
\]
The query is excluded by ID. In the core exact search, similarities are computed in 64-bit blocks and rounded to twelve decimal places for deterministic tie resolution by global row order. Independent sorting checks verify the stored lists. The combinatorial reference for independent uniform lists among $M$ eligible candidates is $k/M$; the adjusted overlap is $(O_k-k/M)/(1-k/M)$. This reference does not remove density, popular-neighbour or composition effects. The primary results therefore retain raw overlap alongside explicitly matched candidate pools.

\subsection{Selection screens and retained alerts}
The original screen uses twenty selections at each of 1,024 and 2,048 articles per Field-period group, paired between models. It flags an absolute median shift above 0.02 or a central 95\% selection width above 0.04 at the larger size. A row permutation per model/group provides an alignment diagnostic rather than a permutation-test $p$-value. The quality sensitivity retains one representative per connected duplicate DOI/text component and excludes specified flags, leaving 448,886 records. It changes the operational population and does not replace the main corpus.

Subfield screens use twenty nested selections up to 256/512 articles, subject to availability. Groups below eight articles lack a two-size diagnosis. If the larger draw equals the full group, its zero selection width is explicitly marked and cannot establish precision for a wider population. Table S4 keeps these cases separate from the original Field-period alerts.

\section{Text input, pooling and truncation}
This experiment keeps the papers fixed and changes what each model reads. The main question is whether that change matters more or less than choosing another model.

\subsection{Model-specific input effects}
The title/abstract experiment uses 52,000 articles: 400 per Field-period group and 2,000 per Field, selected by a fixed ID-based order. The first trial used 26,000; all those IDs remain in the larger set. For each model, the model-change effect is one minus mean agreement with the other nine models on title plus abstract. The input-change effect is one minus agreement between that model's combined input and its title-only or abstract-only input. Their difference is positive when model change is larger. Neighbour changes use the same 2,000 candidates per Field; self is excluded.

A symmetric summary of input transitions checks dependence on the combined-input reference. Mean, CLS and SEP scenarios cross all three inputs; only the four word-level BERTs change readout, which also changes the cross-model reference. Saved combined-input and pilot vectors were reused with exact ID/text alignment and bitwise checks. The analysis makes a practical comparison of input conditions, not a causal decomposition of architecture and content.

\section{Geometric properties and construct checks}
These checks ask what a numerical property actually describes. A measure can repeat reliably and still fail to distinguish separate groups from an elongated cloud or a few extreme points.

\subsection{Properties, alternatives and selection stability}
The geometric analysis reuses the 52,000-article input panel, with 2,000 articles per Field and equal period counts. All vectors have unit length and calculations use 64-bit arithmetic. For distinct vectors $u_i,u_j$, angular spread is the median of $\arccos(u_i^\top u_j)$, reported in degrees. Alternatives are its 10th/90th percentiles and the median angle to the centre direction. They describe spread rather than semantic diversity or isotropy \citep{rudman-etal-2022-isoscore}.

After centring the unit-vector cloud, let $\lambda_j$ be the covariance eigenvalues and $p_j=\lambda_j/\sum_l\lambda_l$. The participation ratio is $\mathrm{PR}=1/\sum_j p_j^2=(\mathrm{tr}\,C)^2/\mathrm{tr}(C^2)$, where $C$ is the centred covariance matrix. This is the main measure of effective linear dimension. The entropy alternative is $\exp(-\sum_j p_j\log p_j)$, applied to the covariance spectrum \citep{roy_2007_40328}. D80 counts the smallest number of descending eigenvalues containing 80\% of variance. These quantities are unchanged by adding zero coordinates, so the 384- versus 768-coordinate comparison is not normalised by ambient dimension. They describe linear variation, not nonlinear intrinsic dimension or a count of topics.

Twenty half samples contain 1,000 articles per Field, and five further equal-size selections contain 2,000, all drawn from the fixed corpus with matched IDs across models. Central 90\% ranges and absolute median shifts are divided by the primary value. Operational thresholds are 5\% for angular spread, 10\% for PR and 20\% for connection. With five repeats, quantiles are coarse; individual values are retained. Passing those selections does not erase the four PR half-sample alerts. The complete analysis contains 16,884 sets of measures, including time, size and representation controls; not every alternative was repeated under every selection.

\subsection{Why connection is not general fragmentation}
Connection uses the second-smallest eigenvalue of the normalised graph Laplacian $L=I-D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}$ on the unweighted union of directed nearest-neighbour links. The value is zero for a disconnected graph. Primary $k=25$ is checked at 10 and 50, with a weighted local-scale graph and mutual-neighbour components as alternatives. Spectral-clustering theory motivates the construction \citep{vonLuxburg2007tutorial}; it does not establish that a small gap means separate scientific themes. Eigenvalue residuals and independent dense calculations were checked.

Single-linkage merging supplies the radii at which the largest component reaches specified fractions of the cloud. Radius ratios and the maximum-to-median minimum-spanning-tree edge ratio provide different connection descriptions. Nine synthetic clouds, followed by Gaussian references and article controls, exposed non-equivalent responses to elongation, isolated outliers and separated components. Fixed $k$ also changes its fraction of candidates as sample size grows. The approximately fixed-fraction comparison was added during execution and is identified as a later sensitivity. These failures are why connection is excluded from the manuscript's interpretation of Field fragmentation.

\section{All Field-pair comparisons}
Here, a pair is simply two different research areas, such as Medicine and Energy. The 325 pairs cover every such comparison among the 26 areas. The calculation asks which area has the larger geometric value, and whether changing the model reverses that answer.

\subsection{Directions and magnitude}
All unordered Field pairs are enumerated by ascending OpenAlex ID. For each model and property, the 26 saved conditions are the primary selection, twenty half samples and five additional equal-size selections, with repeat indices matched across the two Fields. If $d_r=2(B_r-A_r)/(A_r+B_r)$, direction is positive when $\min_r d_r>t+10^{-10}$ and negative when $\max_r d_r<-t-10^{-10}$. Otherwise it is unresolved. The minima $t\in\{0,0.01,0.05,0.10\}$ were specified before the pair counts, after the morphology results. They are magnitude sensitivities, not tests of significance.

All-ten agreement requires every model to have the same persistent direction. Opposition requires at least one persistently positive and one persistently negative model. All remaining pairs are unresolved, including partial agreement. These classes are exhaustive and disjoint. The full range, rather than a favourable central interval, determines persistence; the overlapping selections do not constitute independent replications.

\subsection{Alternative measures and model panels}
An opposing model pair is retained under the alternatives only if those same two models preserve their original directions for every available alternative. Angular alternatives have 26 conditions; spectral alternatives have three (primary, first half sample and first additional selection). D80 is discrete and ties are unresolved. At positive minima, the principal property must exceed the minimum, while the alternatives must retain direction. Models are never replaced with different opposing models simply to preserve a count. Ten leave-one-model panels and the six similarity-oriented models test panel dependence; the smaller panel naturally offers fewer opportunities for opposition.
